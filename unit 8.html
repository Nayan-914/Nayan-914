Chapter 8: Information Security and Cyber Law

1. What is Information Technology? Describe it with its social impacts.

Information Technology (IT) is a modern concept for communication and information flow. It plays a vital role in education, industry, healthcare, research, and entertainment. While IT has positive impacts such as enhancing communication and facilitating learning, it also has negative aspects, including cyber crimes like data theft and hacking.

Information Security

Information security refers to protecting network infrastructure from unauthorized access, misuse, modification, or destruction. It focuses on:

  • Confidentiality: Ensuring information is accessible only to authorized individuals.
  • Integrity: Maintaining accuracy and completeness of data.
  • Availability: Ensuring information is available whenever needed.

Cryptography

Cryptography is the science of secure communication through encryption and decryption.

2. What is Digital Divide?

Digital Divide refers to the gap between people with and without access to technology, often seen between urban and rural areas or rich and poor.

3. What are the computer ethics or cyber ethics?

Cyber ethics are moral guidelines for responsible computer usage:

  • Do not steal or misuse others’ information.
  • Do not spread viruses or hack systems.
  • Do not pirate software or misuse electronic transactions.

4. What is cyber crime? List some examples.

Cyber crime involves illegal activities using computers or the internet. Examples include:

  • Hacking
  • Spreading viruses
  • Stealing information
  • Software piracy
  • Identity theft

5. What is Cyber Law?

Cyber law governs the use of computers and the internet. Nepal's cyber law includes:

  • Electronic and Digital Signature Law

B. Short Answer Questions

1. What challenges are we facing in creating a digital society in the Nepalese context?

Nepal faces several challenges in transitioning into a fully digital society. One of the major challenges is the lack of proper infrastructure, especially in rural areas, where access to high-speed internet and modern technology is still limited. Additionally, digital literacy remains a concern, as a significant portion of the population is not well-versed in using digital platforms effectively.

Another pressing issue is cybersecurity threats, with increasing cases of hacking, phishing, and other cybercrimes due to a lack of awareness and inadequate security measures. Furthermore, Nepal suffers from legal and policy gaps, as existing cyber laws are not comprehensive enough, and their enforcement is weak. Financial constraints also play a role, as many individuals and businesses cannot afford the latest technological advancements. Lastly, frequent power outages and unstable internet connectivity further hinder the country's digital transformation.

2. What is computer ethics? What are the commandments of computer ethics?

Computer ethics refers to the moral guidelines and principles that govern the use of computers and digital technology. It involves responsible behavior while using computers, ensuring privacy, security, and fairness in digital interactions.

The Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics, introduced by the Computer Ethics Institute, provide a set of ethical guidelines for computer users:

  • Do not use computers to harm others.
  • Do not interfere with other people’s computer work.
  • Do not snoop around in other people’s files.
  • Do not use computers to steal.
  • Do not use computers to bear false witness (spread misinformation).
  • Do not use or copy software for which you have not paid.
  • Do not use other people’s computer resources without authorization.
  • Do not appropriate others' intellectual output.
  • Always consider the social consequences of the programs and systems you design.
  • Always use computers in ways that show respect and consideration for others.

3. Discuss the role of computer ethics in the formation of a secure and reliable digital society.

Computer ethics plays a crucial role in ensuring a secure and reliable digital society. Ethical practices help maintain trust and integrity in digital transactions and communications. For instance, ensuring data privacy and security prevents unauthorized access and misuse of personal information.

Additionally, adherence to ethical principles reduces cybercrimes such as hacking, online fraud, and identity theft. Ethical computing also promotes fairness and equality, ensuring that digital technologies are used to benefit society rather than exploit vulnerable individuals. Moreover, respecting intellectual property rights and avoiding plagiarism encourage innovation and creativity. By following ethical guidelines, individuals and organizations contribute to a safer and more responsible digital environment.

4. Explain the basic principles/components of information security.

Information security is based on three fundamental principles, commonly known as the CIA Triad:

  • Confidentiality: Ensures that information is accessible only to authorized users. This prevents unauthorized access and protects sensitive data. Encryption, strong passwords, and access controls help maintain confidentiality.
  • Integrity: Ensures that data remains accurate and unaltered. It prevents unauthorized modifications, ensuring that the information is reliable. Checksums, hashing, and secure data transmission methods help maintain integrity.
  • Availability: Ensures that authorized users have access to information and resources when needed. Protection against hardware failures, cyberattacks, and other disruptions ensures high availability of data and services.

5. Define cyber law. What are the major fields of cyber law?

Cyber law refers to the legal framework that governs digital interactions, the internet, and cyberspace. It includes laws related to cybercrimes, online transactions, digital privacy, and intellectual property rights in the digital world.

The major fields of cyber law include:

  • Cybercrime Law: Deals with offenses such as hacking, phishing, identity theft, and online fraud.
  • Data Protection and Privacy Law: Ensures individuals' personal data is protected and not misused.
  • Intellectual Property Law: Protects digital copyrights, trademarks, and patents.
  • E-commerce Law: Regulates online business transactions, digital contracts, and consumer rights.
  • Cybersecurity Law: Focuses on measures to protect networks, systems, and data from cyber threats.

6. How do you know that your computer has been a victim of a malware attack?

A computer infected with malware exhibits several warning signs. Unexpected system slowdowns and frequent crashes indicate the presence of harmful software. Additionally, unusual pop-ups and ads, even when not browsing the internet, suggest malware infection.

Another sign is unauthorized changes in system settings or missing files, indicating that malware is manipulating the system. If a computer suddenly experiences high CPU usage and overheating, it may be running malicious background processes. Finally, unknown applications or programs running automatically are a strong indication of malware presence.

B. Long Answer Questions

1. Explain at least ten activities that are considered as cybercrime.

Answer:

Cybercrimes are criminal activities that involve the use of computers or the internet. Here are ten common activities that are considered as cybercrime:

  • Hacking: Unauthorized access to a computer system or network to steal data or cause harm.
  • Phishing: A fraudulent attempt to obtain sensitive information like passwords, credit card details, or bank account numbers by impersonating a legitimate entity.
  • Identity Theft: Stealing personal information (e.g., Social Security numbers, addresses, credit card information) to commit fraud or other crimes.
  • Cyberstalking: The use of the internet or other electronic means to stalk or harass someone.
  • Malware Distribution: The act of spreading malicious software, such as viruses, worms, or ransomware, to damage or exploit devices and networks.
  • Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks: Overloading a system or network with traffic to make it unavailable to legitimate users.
  • Cyberbullying: Using digital platforms to bully or harass individuals, often with harmful intent.
  • Online Fraud: Scams or fraudulent activities conducted over the internet, such as fake online shopping or investment schemes.
  • Data Breach: Unauthorized access to confidential data, often leading to the exposure or theft of personal, financial, or sensitive information.
  • Intellectual Property Theft: Using, copying, or distributing digital content such as software, music, movies, or books without permission from the owner.

Reasoning: Cybercrimes can harm individuals, organizations, and even governments. These activities can lead to financial losses, data theft, emotional harm, and loss of trust in digital platforms.

2. What do you mean by malicious software? Explain any ten malicious software attacks.

Answer:

Malicious software, commonly known as malware, refers to any software intentionally designed to cause damage to computers, servers, or networks. Malware can perform harmful actions like stealing data, corrupting files, or controlling the system.

  • Viruses: Malicious programs that attach themselves to files or programs and spread to other files when executed, damaging or corrupting data.
  • Worms: Self-replicating programs that spread across networks, often without the need for human intervention, leading to network congestion or system crashes.
  • Trojan Horses: Malicious software that pretends to be harmless or beneficial to trick users into installing it, which then allows attackers to steal data or take control of the system.
  • Ransomware: Software that encrypts a user’s files or locks them out of their system, demanding payment (ransom) to restore access.
  • Spyware: Programs that secretly monitor the user’s activities and send the information back to the attacker without the user’s knowledge.
  • Adware: Software that automatically displays unwanted ads and tracks user behavior to deliver targeted advertisements.
  • Rootkits: Software tools that allow attackers to gain unauthorized access to a system and hide their presence, often controlling the system at a deep level.
  • Keyloggers: Programs that record keystrokes made by a user, often used to steal sensitive information like passwords and credit card numbers.
  • Botnets: A network of compromised computers that are controlled remotely to carry out malicious activities such as DDoS attacks, spamming, or spreading malware.
  • Backdoors: Hidden methods or vulnerabilities within software that allow unauthorized access to a system or network, often used by hackers to bypass security measures.

Reasoning: Malicious software poses a severe risk to digital systems, potentially leading to data loss, financial theft, privacy breaches, and operational disruption.

3. Explain intellectual property along with its types in detail. What legal provisions do we have in Nepal to preserve intellectual property rights?

Answer:

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the mind, such as inventions, designs, literary and artistic works, symbols, names, and images used in commerce. IP rights allow creators to control and profit from their creations, thereby encouraging innovation and creativity.

Types of Intellectual Property:

  • Patents: A right granted to an inventor for a new invention, giving the inventor exclusive rights to the invention for a certain period (usually 20 years).
  • Copyrights: Protect original works of authorship, such as books, music, films, and software, providing creators exclusive rights to reproduce and distribute their works.
  • Trademarks: Symbols, names, or other distinctive identifiers that distinguish goods or services from others in the market.
  • Trade Secrets: Confidential business information, such as formulas, processes, or strategies, that provide a business advantage and are protected by keeping them secret.
  • Design Rights: Protect the visual design of products, including their shape, texture, or color, ensuring that the design is unique and not copied.

Legal Provisions in Nepal to Protect Intellectual Property:

  • Copyright Act 2059 (2002): Governs copyright protection, including works of literature, music, and other artistic creations.
  • Patent, Design, and Trademark Act 2022 (1975): Covers patents, industrial designs, and trademarks, providing legal protection to inventors and creators.
  • Trade Secrets Protection: Although there is no specific act for trade secrets, they are generally protected through contracts and non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).

Reasoning: Intellectual property laws help creators protect their work and prevent others from exploiting their creations without permission, fostering a culture of innovation and creativity.

4. Explain digital signature along with its working mechanism.

Answer:

A digital signature is an electronic equivalent of a handwritten signature or a stamped seal but much more secure. It is used to verify the authenticity and integrity of digital messages or documents. It ensures that the sender is who they claim to be and that the message has not been tampered with during transmission.

Working Mechanism of Digital Signature:

  • Message Digest: A hash function is applied to the message to generate a fixed-length string (message digest), which uniquely represents the message content.
  • Encryption: The sender uses their private key to encrypt the message digest. This encrypted hash value becomes the digital signature.
  • Transmission: The original message, along with the digital signature, is sent to the receiver.
  • Verification: The receiver uses the sender’s public key to decrypt the digital signature. This reveals the message digest.
  • Integrity Check: The receiver applies the hash function to the received message and compares the result with the decrypted message digest. If they match, the message is authentic and unchanged.

Reasoning: Digital signatures provide a high level of security, as they rely on cryptographic techniques, ensuring that both the message’s origin and integrity are verifiable.

5. Explain any ten policies included in ICT Policy 2072.

Answer:

  • Digital Infrastructure Development: Establishing high-speed broadband infrastructure to ensure universal access to the internet across the country.
  • E-Governance: Promoting the use of ICT in government services to increase efficiency, transparency, and accountability in public administration.
  • Cybersecurity: Strengthening measures to protect against cyber threats and ensuring the safe use of the internet for citizens and businesses.
  • ICT Literacy: Increasing digital literacy among the population, especially in rural areas, to bridge the digital divide.
  • Research and Development: Encouraging innovation and the development of new ICT technologies by supporting research and development activities.
  • Digital Economy: Promoting e-commerce and digital businesses, supporting start-ups, and fostering a vibrant digital economy.
  • Public-Private Partnerships: Encouraging collaborations between the government and private sector to build ICT infrastructure and services.
  • Cloud Computing: Promoting the use of cloud technology for storing and sharing data efficiently and securely.
  • ICT for Education: Incorporating ICT tools in the education system to improve teaching and learning.
  • E-Health: Developing ICT-based solutions for the health sector to improve healthcare delivery, including telemedicine and health information systems.

Reasoning: The ICT Policy 2072 is designed to harness the potential of ICT to drive socio-economic development, enhance public services, and improve the quality of life for citizens in Nepal.
